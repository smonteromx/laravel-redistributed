# Identity system branch

Read this reference when the scope includes an identity system.

## Establish the identity brief

Capture the product's purpose, audience, category, promise, personality, naming status, geography and culture, languages, legal constraints, channels, production limits, and existing equity. Separate fixed inputs from open decisions.

Translate the evidence into 3–5 direction criteria that can reject weak concepts. Use criteria such as institutional trust, distinctiveness, warmth, legibility, cultural specificity, technical reproduction, or international readability only when the brief supports them.

Done when every proposed direction can be evaluated against the same product-specific criteria.

## Define the identity mood

Treat mood as an operational atmosphere, not a decorative label. Reuse an established brand mood when present; otherwise follow the selection process in `SKILL.md`.

Define the chosen mood with:

- a memorable name or short phrase;
- concrete sensory, material, cultural, or environmental references;
- the emotional range it should sustain;
- translation cues for color, typography, form, imagery, motion, and voice;
- restraints that keep the expression coherent across channels.

Done when two reviewers can evaluate a proposed identity choice against the mood and reach a comparable verdict.

## Select the mark architecture

Use descriptive behavior together with regional terminology; naming conventions vary.

| Route | Construction | Strong fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wordmark / logotype | The name is the mark | Distinctive or short names; typography can carry recognition |
| Symbol / isotype | A standalone symbol | Recognition must survive without the name; the brand can build symbol equity |
| Combination mark / imagotype | Symbol and wordmark can separate | Flexible responsive systems and many channels |
| Integrated mark / isologo | Name and symbol form one inseparable unit | Compact signatures whose meaning depends on the fused composition |
| Monogram or lettermark | Initials become the primary mark | Long names, constrained spaces, or established initials |
| Emblem | Type lives inside a containing form | Seals, certification, heritage, uniforms, or physical applications |
| Mascot or character | A character carries recognition | Community, education, entertainment, or brands that need an expressive narrator |

Evaluate at least name length, recognition without text, smallest use, monochrome use, multilingual behavior, responsive signatures, motion, embroidery/print constraints, app icon/favicon needs, and future sub-brands. Recommend the strongest route; ask the user when preference is material or routes remain tied.

Done when the selected route survives the required channels and discarded routes have a short rationale.

## Structure the identity delivery

Include only the relevant sections:

1. **Brand frame:** purpose, positioning, audience, promise, attributes, selected mood, and direction criteria.
2. **Naming system:** canonical name, descriptor, pronunciation, capitalization, accessibility text, and sub-brand rules.
3. **Signature architecture:** chosen mark type, symbol/wordmark relationship, responsive variants, and lockups.
4. **Construction:** geometry, spacing, alignment, minimum size, optical corrections, and clearspace.
5. **Variants:** primary, reversed, monochrome, small-size, app icon/favicon, print-safe, and background-specific recipes.
6. **Color and type:** identity roles, on-color pairings, font licensing/fallbacks, and typographic voice.
7. **Expression:** imagery, illustration, iconography, patterns, motion, sound, or verbal voice only where the brand needs them.
8. **Application proofs:** realistic high-risk channels rather than decorative mockups.
9. **Assets and handoff:** editable masters, exports, naming, color profiles, accessibility labels, and usage mapping.

Test the mark on actual constraints before polishing a presentation board. A compelling hero lockup is insufficient evidence for favicon, monochrome, email, signage, or low-quality print use.

## Keep the target product original

Generate form from the product's story and constraints. Require a fresh rationale for every motif and signature device; replace any choice that cannot be defended from the target brief.

Done when a reviewer can explain why this identity belongs to this product from its own evidence.
